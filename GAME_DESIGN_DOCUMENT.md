# Cast & Crank — Gate 1 GDD

Cast & Crank is a tactile freshwater fishing game for a phone held flat on the palm. Pick a location, cock back and snap forward to cast, feel a bite, make a smaller backward cock to set the hook, then pull by holding the rod raised and tilt it forward/down to ease the line while managing tension. Android gameplay is sensor-only after calibration; touch is reserved for settings and the caught-card reset.

Gate 1 includes Pine Lake and Bluegill only. The finished loop uses `READY → CAST_ARMED → LINE_OUT → BITE → HOOK_WINDOW → REELING → CAUGHT|ESCAPED`. Bluegill fights lightly, bites after 2.4 seconds, and measures 14–31 cm. Pine Lake’s later family is Bluegill, Largemouth Bass, and Channel Catfish; Cedar River (later) contains Rainbow Trout, Smallmouth Bass, and Northern Pike.

The slice favors accessible, forgiving timing: 1.5 seconds to hook and 1.25 continuous seconds in red tension before loss. After a hook, the raised pose becomes the pull reference; the first deliberate forward/down lower captures a relief reference. Gravity position between those poses maps continuously to rod load: high/raised load advances the fish quickly but raises tension, and low/forward load relieves it. Phase changes are guidance markers, not one-shot progress permissions. Bluegill is tuned for a controlled pull/ease rhythm of roughly 12–14 seconds (within the 10–20 second target); sustained full pull resolves quickly by landing or breaking the line. On a first run the player rests the phone flat while it settles, then performs two automatic practice casts; their shared axis, peaks, timing, gyro energy, and noise tolerance become the saved profile. Faster accepted snaps cast farther (8–40 m). Desktop keyboard/test simulation keeps the greybox reviewable without sensors, but Android has no gameplay tap fallback. Future location selection and collection screens are intentionally deferred until the Gate 1 direction is approved.

Eyes-up haptics communicate the fight without requiring the player to stare at a meter. The bite is a two-pulse cue and a successful hook gives one short confirmation before the first fish phrase begins. Each planned fish definition has its own pulse phrase and phrase cadence with deliberate quiet space; in this Gate 1 runtime, Bluegill is the active specimen. Tension at 65% overrides species rhythm with a universal high-warning cadence, and 90% uses a faster universal urgent red-warning cadence; lowering tension restores the fish rhythm. A caught fish gives a three-part lift cue, while an escape is a single low cue. Disabling haptics stops and clears all queued feedback. These six signatures are data and test coverage only until future locations and species pass content approval.
