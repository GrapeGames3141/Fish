# Cast & Crank — Gate 1 GDD

Cast & Crank is a tactile freshwater fishing game for a phone held flat on the palm. Pick a location, cock back and snap forward to cast, feel a bite, make a smaller backward cock to set the hook, then pull by holding the rod raised and tilt it forward/down to ease the line while managing tension. Android gameplay is sensor-only after calibration; touch is reserved for settings and the caught-card reset.

The expansion activates Pine Lake (Bluegill, Largemouth Bass, Channel Catfish) and Cedar River (Rainbow Trout, Smallmouth Bass, Northern Pike). Each location uses a deterministic weighted table with distinct size range, bite delay, fight strength, and eyes-up haptic phrase. The location picker and records are available only outside an active cast/fight. Bluegill keeps its approved illustration; non-Bluegill catches deliberately use a code-native tinted silhouette until an approved art family exists.

The slice favors accessible, forgiving timing: 1.8 seconds to hook and 1.25 continuous seconds in red tension before loss. Right-handed default is cock/pull/hook right and snap/ease left; the optional left-handed setting mirrors that contract and deliberately clears calibration. Runtime recognition uses the learned 3D profile axis plus a polarity guard, while baseline noise is measured only during deliberate settling. Two practice casts produce the robust lower gyro reference; accepted examples tick haptically. After a hook, gravity maps through a small deadband and roughly 100ms response to continuous rod load: pull advances the fish but raises tension, ease relieves it. Bluegill targets 10–20 seconds of controlled pull/ease. Desktop keyboard simulation remains review-only; Android has no gameplay touch fallback.

Eyes-up haptics communicate the fight without requiring the player to stare at a meter. The bite is a two-pulse cue and a successful hook gives one short confirmation before the first fish phrase begins. Each planned fish definition has its own pulse phrase and phrase cadence with deliberate quiet space; in this Gate 1 runtime, Bluegill is the active specimen. Tension at 65% overrides species rhythm with a universal high-warning cadence, and 90% uses a faster universal urgent red-warning cadence; lowering tension restores the fish rhythm. A caught fish gives a three-part lift cue, while an escape is a single low cue. Disabling haptics stops and clears all queued feedback. These six signatures are data and test coverage only until future locations and species pass content approval.
